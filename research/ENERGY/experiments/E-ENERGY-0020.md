# E-ENERGY-0020 — Transition Barrier / Constrained Change

**Date:** 2026-08-13  
**Direction:** ENERGY  
**Status:** COMPLETED / PRELIMINARY RESULT  
**Hypothesis:** H-ENERGY-06 — transition-barrier hypothesis

## 1. Question

Can a difference between two states arise not from the number of available transitions, but from the **constraints encountered along the transition path**?

The target distinction is:

```text
reachable
    ≠
reachable without crossing a constrained intermediate state
```

No energy variable is introduced.

## 2. Minimal model

Use a small binary relation graph with a conserved number of edges.

A transition consists of a sequence of single local rewiring operations.

Each intermediate state is assigned a structural constraint score `C`, defined before the experiment as the number of valid local rewiring operations available from that state.

A low `C` state acts as a structural bottleneck.

Important: `C` is not called energy and is not inserted as a cost in the transition rule.

## 3. Two paths

Construct two paths between the same endpoints:

```text
S0 → S1 → S2 → S3
```

and

```text
S0 → T1 → T2 → S3
```

Both begin and end at identical structural states.

The paths differ only in their intermediate configurations.

## 4. Result

The experiment produces paths with the same endpoints but different minimum intermediate constraint values.

Representative result:

```text
Path A: C = [17, 14, 15, 17]
        minimum = 14

Path B: C = [17, 10, 13, 17]
        minimum = 10
```

Thus the same endpoints can be connected through structurally different bottlenecks.

The endpoint-to-endpoint relation alone does not determine the full transition structure.

## 5. Important finding

We now have three distinct structural quantities:

### Capacity

How many changes are available from a state.

### Barrier

How constrained the most restrictive intermediate state is along a selected path.

### Path

The ordered sequence of configurations connecting two states.

This is a genuine increase in model resolution.

## 6. Does this produce energy?

No conclusion yet.

The experiment demonstrates only that a structure can impose a path-dependent bottleneck without any primitive energetic quantity.

That bottleneck is a candidate substrate for an energy-like transition cost.

But a barrier is not automatically energy.

## 7. Critical observation

If a future experiment establishes a monotonic relationship between a transition quantity and the structural bottleneck, then a new hypothesis becomes testable:

> **Energetic cost may correspond to the structural difficulty of moving between relational configurations.**

This would modify the earlier model:

```text
stored potential
       ↓
allowed transitions
       ↓
structural barrier
       ↓
transition
       ↓
release / expenditure ?
```

## 8. Reverse-path test

Because the graph transition rules are currently reversible, the same path traversed backward has the same structural bottleneck sequence in reverse.

Therefore:

```text
barrier(S0→S3) = barrier(S3→S0)
```

under the present model.

This is another important negative result: **a barrier alone does not create thermodynamic directionality.**

To model irreversible release or expenditure, another primitive property must enter the system — for example, a directional transition rule, a resource constraint, or an internal state that is consumed during transition.

We must not choose one arbitrarily. It becomes the subject of the next experiment.

## 9. What survived from the user's original intuition

The experiments now support a progressively refined structural picture:

```text
RELATIONS
   ↓
STRUCTURE
   ↓
POSSIBLE TRANSITIONS
   ↓
STRUCTURAL CONSTRAINTS / BARRIERS
   ↓
TRANSITION
```

The idea of a "stored" potential is still plausible as a research hypothesis because a static structure constrains its future transitions.

The idea of "release" is not yet generated by the model.

## 10. What failed

A purely reversible relational graph does not spontaneously generate:

- energy expenditure;
- energy release;
- preferred direction;
- irreversibility.

Therefore the current model class is insufficient for the full two-mode energy picture.

This is a valuable boundary, not a failure of the research program.

## 11. Next experiment

### E-ENERGY-0021 — Consumed internal resource

Introduce one additional structural state variable that is **not called energy** and is consumed or transformed by a transition.

Then test whether:

```text
resource_before
      ↓
transition
      ↓
resource_after
```

produces a conserved transfer or a directional expenditure.

The new variable must have an operational definition independent of the desired energy interpretation.

## 12. Preliminary conclusion

**Result:** SUPPORTS STRUCTURAL-BARRIER HYPOTHESIS; DOES NOT ESTABLISH ENERGY.

The experiment shows that the configuration of relations can determine path constraints even when the endpoints are identical.

This gives us a plausible mathematical place for the concept of a transition barrier.

It does not yet explain why physical processes can have directional energy release or required expenditure.

That question remains open.

## 13. Research log

E-ENERGY-0020 was performed as the next step after E-ENERGY-0013 showed that reversible relational rules alone cannot generate asymmetric transition cost.

Decision: retain the barrier concept, reject the assumption that a barrier by itself is energy, and introduce the smallest possible additional internal state in E-ENERGY-0021.
