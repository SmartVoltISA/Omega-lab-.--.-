# E-ENERGY-0036 — Emergence of Three States Without Color Labels

**Date:** 2026-08-23
**Direction:** ENERGY / RELATIONAL DYNAMICS
**Status:** COMPLETED / NEGATIVE CONTROL
**Parent:** E-ENERGY-0035

## Question
Can a continuous memory-bearing system spontaneously separate into three stable classes without defining RED/GREEN/BLUE in advance?

## Protocol
A continuous scalar state x was driven through repeated positive/negative cycles. The update included current input and persistence of the previous state. No color labels or ternary state labels were supplied.

Tested persistence values: m = 0, 0.5, 1.0, 1.5, 2.0.

The resulting state distribution was inspected for stable clusters rather than assigned colors beforehand.

## Result
The minimal scalar model did **not** spontaneously generate three stable classes.

Instead it produced two persistent extreme states, with the central region mainly representing transition rather than a third stable attractor.

Representative occupancy for m=0:
- negative extreme: ~40.8%
- central region: ~18.4%
- positive extreme: ~40.8%

With increasing persistence the central occupancy decreased strongly while the two extreme states became more dominant:

m=0.5: central ~10%
m=1.0: central ~2.8%
m=1.5: central ~1.2%
m=2.0: central ~0.6%

## Interpretation
This is an important negative result.

The three-color interpretation cannot yet be claimed as an emergent universal property of the minimal memory model. A simple continuous hysteretic variable naturally gives two persistent polar states plus a transition region.

Therefore:

> RED / GREEN / BLUE should not be treated as three fundamental states until a model produces three attractors without imposing them.

The earlier E-ENERGY-0035 result remains valid only as a demonstration that a three-state memory encoding can represent history.

## Consequence for the Ω hypothesis
If three genuine states are fundamental, an additional structural ingredient is required beyond a single scalar memory variable. Candidates include:

- at least two coupled relational degrees of freedom;
- competition/frustration between relations;
- conservation plus topology;
- a non-linear multi-well relational potential;
- spatial coupling.

The next test should therefore move to a coupled spatial graph rather than artificially forcing three states.
