# E-ENERGY-0026 — Coupled Potential Transfer

**Date:** 2026-08-13  
**Direction:** ENERGY  
**Status:** COMPLETED / IMPORTANT NEGATIVE RESULT  
**Parent:** E-ENERGY-0025  
**Hypothesis:** H-ENERGY-09 — relational potential as a third observable

## 1. Question

Can two coupled subsystems spontaneously redistribute a candidate potential so that one decreases while the other increases, with the combined quantity remaining invariant, without hard-coding the balance?

Target:

```text
A_high + B_low
       ↓
   coupled dynamics
       ↓
A_low + B_high
```

## 2. Constraint

No primitive energy variable.

No explicit balancing rule such as `A -= 1; B += 1`.

The only allowed operations are local relational updates generated by the coupled graph.

## 3. Candidate observables tested

The experiment tracks:

- retained-history measure `M`;
- immediate transition capacity `T`;
- minimum transition barrier `B`;
- combined candidate functions of `M`, `T`, and `B`.

None is declared to be energy.

## 4. Result

Coupled dynamics can produce redistribution of local structural/history descriptors, but no single scalar constructed from the tested descriptors remains invariant across all coupled transitions.

Some transitions show a transfer-like pattern:

```text
A decreases
B increases
```

but other allowed transitions produce:

```text
A decreases
B unchanged
```

or

```text
A changes
B changes in a non-compensating way
```

Therefore the apparent conservation observed in earlier hand-matched examples does not emerge universally from the current dynamics.

## 5. Major negative result

The current model does **not** spontaneously produce a conserved transferable quantity merely from:

```text
memory + capacity + barrier
```

This is an important falsification of the strongest version of H-ENERGY-09 within this model class.

## 6. What survives

The following remains supported within the model:

```text
RELATIONAL CHANGE
      ↓
PRESERVED HISTORY
      ↓
MODIFIED FUTURE POSSIBILITIES
      ↓
STRUCTURAL BARRIERS
```

But the step:

```text
      ↓
UNIVERSALLY CONSERVED TRANSFERABLE QUANTITY
```

has not emerged.

## 7. Consequence for the accumulator hypothesis

The computational analogue of an accumulator can be constructed, but it does not arise automatically from arbitrary relational dynamics.

Therefore the user's accumulator intuition is currently divided into two propositions:

### Proposition A

A structure can possess a stored state that constrains future transformations.

**Supported computationally.**

### Proposition B

That stored state is necessarily a conserved transferable quantity with energy-like behaviour.

**Not supported by the present model.**

## 8. Important pivot

At this point, simply adding more graph complexity would be the wrong move.

The experiment indicates that the missing ingredient is not another arbitrary structural descriptor.

The next question should be:

> **What minimal rule makes a stored potential transferable while preserving a total, without simply writing conservation into the rule?**

This is now a search for a minimal conservation-generating mechanism.

## 9. Candidate mechanisms to test separately

Do not combine them yet.

### C1 — Complementary state

Two local states are constrained so that a transformation in one necessarily changes the other.

### C2 — Shared conserved relation

A relation exists between subsystems whose state cannot be changed locally without compensating change elsewhere.

### C3 — Reversible bookkeeping

The full transition is bijective, with the candidate quantity emerging as an invariant of the transition permutation.

### C4 — Resource topology

The graph itself limits the total amount of a particular transition-capable structure.

Each mechanism must be tested independently.

## 10. Next experiment

### E-ENERGY-0027 — Minimal Conservation Generator

Construct the smallest relational system in which a conserved quantity could emerge from the transition rules themselves.

Start with two coupled subsystems and one shared constraint.

Do not define the conserved quantity in advance.

Enumerate the complete state-transition graph and search for nontrivial invariants.

The invariant must:

1. hold for every allowed transition in the closed system;
2. be nonconstant across the state space;
3. permit redistribution between subsystems;
4. not be equivalent merely to node count, edge count, or a fixed structural constant.

## 11. Preliminary conclusion

**Result:** IMPORTANT NEGATIVE RESULT.

The current relational-history model does not spontaneously produce a universal conserved transferable quantity.

This does not reject the energy hypothesis globally. It rejects the claim that the currently tested ingredients are sufficient.

The research therefore moves from "find the quantity" to:

> **Find the minimal structural condition under which a conserved transferable quantity can emerge.**
