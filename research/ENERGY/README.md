## 0. Purpose

This branch investigates whether **energy can be understood as a consequence of change in relations within a structure**.

The branch is deliberately separated from classical definitions at the first stage. Classical physics is used as a validation and falsification reference, not as an assumption that the proposed ontology is correct.

The working rule is the Ω-Lab rule:

> **Do not insert the target phenomenon into the model before the model has produced an observable that can be tested against it.**

Therefore the first task is not to prove that energy is relational. The first task is to determine whether a minimal relational system can generate an invariant or measurable quantity with properties sufficiently analogous to physical energy.

## D-ENERGY-01 — Energy from relational change

### Initial formulation

> **Energy is a consequence of a change in a relation in space.**

This is the user's original working formulation and is preserved exactly as the starting hypothesis.

### More conservative research formulation

> **Energy may be a measurable quantity associated with changes in relations between states of a system.**

The second formulation is NOT a replacement for the first. It is a temporary operational form used to make the hypothesis testable without prematurely claiming that the ontology is established.

# 4. Current hypotheses

## H-ENERGY-01 — Relational-change hypothesis

**Statement:**

> A change in the relation between elements of a system is sufficient to produce a measurable quantity that can serve as a candidate representation of energy.

**Status:** OPEN

**Evidence:** none yet from a dedicated Ω-Lab experiment.

**Counterevidence:** not yet systematically tested.

**Required test:** construct the smallest possible relational model and determine whether an energy-like invariant can be derived from change without inserting energy as a primitive variable.

---

## H-ENERGY-02 — Spatial relation hypothesis

**Statement:**

> If physical energy is fundamentally relational, at least some energy phenomena should be reproducible from changes in spatial relations or spatial configurations.

**Status:** OPEN

**Risk:** the formulation may be too narrow because fields, radiation, and quantum systems cannot safely be reduced at this stage to simple pairwise spatial distances.

**Required test:** compare a distance-based model with a more general relation-based model.

---

## H-ENERGY-03 — Energy as secondary observable

**Statement:**

> Energy should not be introduced as a primitive in the minimal model; it should emerge as an observable/invariant of state transitions or relational transformations.

**Status:** OPEN

**Falsification condition:** if every successful model requires an explicitly inserted energy variable or equivalent hidden energy assumption, the emergence claim is weakened or rejected for that model class.

# 5. Initial examples for validation

These examples are **validation targets, not evidence for the hypothesis**.

## 5.1 Gravitational potential / kinetic transformation

A body changes its spatial relation to the Earth while falling. Classical physics describes a transformation from gravitational potential energy to kinetic energy.

Ω-Lab question:

> Can the transformation be described first as a change in relational configuration and only secondarily mapped onto the conventional energy description?

Expected use: conceptual bridge, not proof.

## 5.2 Oscillatory system

A pendulum or ideal spring repeatedly changes its configuration.

Ω-Lab question:

> Does repeated relational change naturally produce a conserved scalar or invariant corresponding to total mechanical energy?

Expected use: test of invariance under repeated state transitions.

## 5.3 Free radiation / field case

A photon carries energy without requiring a simple two-body spatial-distance interpretation.

Ω-Lab question:

> If the two-body spatial relation is insufficient, does a generalized relation between field states provide a better substrate?

This is a deliberate pressure test against H-ENERGY-02.

# 7. Proposed experiment sequence

## E-ENERGY-0001 — Minimal relation change

**Purpose:** determine whether changing one relation produces a nontrivial measurable quantity without defining energy in advance.

### Model

```text
A —— R —— B
```

where `R` is the relation state.

Possible minimal representation:

```text
R ∈ {0,1,2,...}
```

or another explicitly declared finite state space.

### Procedure

1. Define the smallest state space.
2. Define one local update rule.
3. Generate a state trace.
4. Record the relation before and after each update.
5. Derive candidate observables from the trace.
6. Test which observables remain stable under irrelevant re-description.
7. Only after this compare candidates with known energy properties.

### Required controls

- static relation with no change;
- reversible change;
- irreversible change;
- repeated cyclic change;
- random change.

### Primary question

> Does relational change produce a nontrivial invariant or conserved quantity without that quantity being explicitly encoded?

### Status

PLANNED — NOT YET RUN.

---

## E-ENERGY-0002 — Reversibility test

**Purpose:** determine whether a candidate energy-like quantity depends on the direction/path of relational change.

Compare:

```text
A → B → A
```

with:

```text
A → C → A
```

and inspect whether the candidate quantity returns to the same value.

### Key distinction

A quantity that merely tracks the number of updates is not sufficient. It must depend on the structural state or transformation in a meaningful way.

### Status

BLOCKED until E-ENERGY-0001 produces a candidate observable.

---

## E-ENERGY-0003 — Conservation test

**Purpose:** determine whether the candidate quantity is conserved under closed relational dynamics.

### Prediction if H-ENERGY-03 is useful

A candidate energy-like quantity should remain invariant under the defined closed update rules, or transform between identifiable components while preserving a total.

### Falsification

If no stable invariant exists across repeated closed transformations, the current emergence hypothesis fails for that model class.

### Status

BLOCKED until E-ENERGY-0001.

---

## E-ENERGY-0004 — Spatial extension

**Purpose:** test whether the minimal relational result survives when relations are embedded in spatial configuration.

Compare:

```text
abstract relation graph
```

against:

```text
spatially embedded relation graph
```

### Question

Is space required to produce the energy-like quantity, or does relational change alone suffice?

### Status

BLOCKED until E-ENERGY-0001.

## E-ENERGY-0005 — Counterexample sweep

**Purpose:** deliberately search for systems where relational change occurs but no energy-like observable exists, and systems where energy exists while the chosen relation variable does not change.

This experiment is essential because confirmation alone is insufficient.

### Required cases

- change without conventional energy transfer;
- energy transfer with apparently unchanged selected relation;
- cyclic systems;
- dissipative systems;
- field/radiative cases.

### Status

PLANNED after initial model class is established.

# 10. Important distinctions

## Change is not automatically energy

A system can change without us having demonstrated an energy quantity.

Therefore:

```text
change ≠ energy
```

is retained as a methodological constraint.

## Conservation is not automatically energy

A conserved quantity can be mathematically constructed for many systems. Conservation alone does not prove physical identity with energy.

Therefore:

```text
invariant ≠ automatically energy
```

## Spatial relation is not automatically fundamental

The word "space" in the initial formulation is itself a possible assumption. The branch must test whether space is fundamental to the proposed relation or merely one representation of a more general structural relation.

# 12. Rules for this branch

1. Do not declare the hypothesis true because it resembles known physics.
2. Do not use conventional energy equations as primitives in the first minimal model.
3. Do not hide energy inside a parameter under another name.
4. Separate observation, interpretation, hypothesis, and conclusion.
5. Preserve failed experiments and rejected hypotheses.
6. Do not modify a frozen experiment after execution; create a new experiment/version instead.
7. Prefer the smallest model capable of falsifying the claim.
8. Test counterexamples deliberately.
9. Do not equate mathematical invariance with physical energy without additional evidence.
10. Do not infer physical time from update order.
11. Do not replace the relation graph with the provenance tree; use both.
12. Every new claim receives a provenance path and epistemic status.
