# TIME-003 — AUTONOMOUS RELATIONAL CONTINUATION

### Inputs forbidden

- time variable;
- timestamp;
- duration;
- clock;
- rate;
- physical unit.

### Inputs allowed

- finite state set;
- deterministic local transition rule;
- recurrence/closure;
- event count only as an ordinal index, not as a physical duration.

### Construction

Two autonomous cyclic processes were defined:

- process A: period 5 states;
- process B: period 7 states.

The first common recurrence occurs at event-count 35, then 70, 105, ... .

### Result

A continuing process does not require a pre-existing physical-time variable in its abstract description. It requires a transition rule and an ordering of successive transitions.

The recurrence coordinate continues indefinitely:

`1, 2, 3, ...`

but remains dimensionless. The system therefore possesses **continuation of change** without yet possessing a physical duration.

### Important boundary

A program loop, graph walk, cellular automaton step, or recurrence can generate succession. Calling its iteration number "time" would add a name, not a physical metric.

# TIME-004 — RELATIONAL DURATION AND SCALE

### Question

Can continuation itself determine an absolute duration?

### Test

The same 0→100 event sequence was assigned four positive scales:

`0.1, 1, 3.7, 100`.

All preserve the exact same ordering and normalized relational position.

### Result

The continuation coordinate has a scale symmetry:

`q → c q`, for any `c > 0`.

Therefore structure alone determines an **ordinal/relational temporal coordinate**, but not its absolute scale.

### Strong result

A duration requires an additional invariant that breaks this scale freedom.

This can be supplied operationally by a clock/frequency standard, or physically by a dynamical metric/parameter that all valid observers can recover consistently.

# TIME-005 — CONTINUATION, MEMORY, AND THE ARROW

### Question

Does continued change automatically produce a direction of time?

### Construction

A 16-state cyclic micro-dynamics was initialized in one concentrated state and evolved by a symmetric local mixing rule. No variable named time was used; only transition iterations were counted.

Shannon entropy of the coarse distribution was measured over iterations.

Observed entropy:

- iteration 0: approximately 0 bits;
- iteration 10: approximately 2.034 bits;
- iteration 50: approximately 3.205 bits;
- iteration 100: approximately 3.659 bits.

Entropy was non-decreasing over the tested trajectory.

### Result

The experiment produces an **arrow-like asymmetry of the observed trajectory** from a low-entropy initial condition under coarse-graining.

But this does NOT mean that entropy created physical time. The transition iteration already supplied an order. The entropy gradient selected a preferred orientation within that ordered sequence.

Therefore:

`ORDER → continuation` does not by itself imply an arrow;

`ORDER + asymmetric state/history conditions → directional arrow` is possible.

This distinction is consistent with the broader physics literature: the thermodynamic arrow is an asymmetry problem distinct from the mere existence of temporal ordering. citeturn0search0

# Current answer to “what is time?”

### Structural answer

> **Time is the relational ordering of distinguishable change, together with a rule for measuring the separation of changes.**

More strictly, the research separates two concepts:

> **Temporal order** is the structure `before/after` generated by ordered change.

> **Duration** is a quantitative measure assigned to that ordered change.

> **Physical time** is the physically realized quantity/metric that makes such durations operationally measurable and comparable.

So the short form is:

`TIME = ORDERED CHANGE + MEASURE`

while:

`ARROW OF TIME = ORDERED CHANGE + an asymmetry selecting an orientation.`

This is the strongest result currently justified by the laboratory. It is NOT a proof that spacetime time is fundamentally emergent in every physical theory.
