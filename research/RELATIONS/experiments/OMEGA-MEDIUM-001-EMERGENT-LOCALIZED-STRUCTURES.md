# Ω-MEDIUM-001 — Emergent Localized Structures

Date: 2026-08-16
Status: COMPLETED PILOT / ELECTRON NOT FOUND / EMERGENT STRUCTURES FOUND

## Question

Can a minimal interaction medium generate a persistent localized structure without naming the target object in advance?

The immediate target was not to force an electron. The target was to determine whether the medium can produce a new stable entity-like pattern from interaction alone, after which the resulting pattern can be compared with physical entities.

## Fundamental constraint

Do not insert:

- electron;
- charge;
- photon;
- spin;
- electromagnetic field;
- particle labels;
- predefined relation alphabet.

The experiment therefore does not claim to model an electron. It tests whether entity-like structures can emerge before physical interpretation.

## Minimal medium

- 1D periodic lattice, N = 101 positions;
- each position contains a binary entity-state: 0 or 1;
- one initial active entity at the center;
- nearest-neighbour interaction only;
- synchronous update;
- no particle labels;
- no physical constants;
- no charge assignment;
- no energy equation;
- no fitted parameters.

The local update rule is an elementary cellular-automaton rule. All 256 possible binary radius-1 local rules were scanned. Therefore rule selection was not made because one rule was known to produce an electron-like object.

## Search criteria

A candidate emergent entity had to remain active and localized for the final 100 of 500 steps, with population <= 25.

A second screen searched for nontrivial localized structures with population 2–10 and measurable persistent displacement.

## Result

64 of 256 rules produced persistent localized activity under the broad criterion. This includes trivial stationary or one-cell persistence and therefore is NOT itself evidence of a particle-like entity.

A stronger displacement screen found 8 rules with persistent localized structures containing 2–10 active sites and nonzero centre-of-mass displacement:

- Rule 14: +1.061 sites/step (periodic-wrap artefact affects the raw COM estimate); direct inspection shows a two-site structure moving one lattice site per step to the left after formation.
- Rule 46: same qualitative class, moving right.
- Rule 84: same qualitative class, moving left.
- Rule 116: same qualitative class, moving left.
- Rule 142: same qualitative class, moving right.
- Rule 174: same qualitative class, moving right.
- Rule 212: same qualitative class, moving left.
- Rule 244: same qualitative class, moving left.

### Direct Rule-14 trace

Starting from one active entity at position 50:

`t=0: {50}`
`t=1: {49,50}`
`t=2: {48,49}`
`t=3: {47,48}`
`t=4: {46,47}`
`t=5: {45,46}`

The pattern therefore becomes a two-site localized structure and then translates one site per step while preserving its two-site population.

## Interpretation

This is a genuine computational observation:

`single entity -> interaction -> two-entity relation -> persistent localized structure -> propagation`

The two active sites are not called an electron. No charge, mass, spin or electromagnetic interaction was supplied. The structure is an emergent dynamical pattern of the chosen medium.

This supports the weaker Ω proposition that a medium with minimal local interaction can generate persistent entity-like structures whose identity is defined by behaviour and relations rather than by a predefined object label.

## Critical limitation

The experiment does NOT show that the emergent glider is an electron.

The cellular automaton rule itself is an imposed dynamical law. The lattice and neighbourhood relation are also imposed. Therefore this is a generative proof-of-concept, not a derivation of fundamental physics.

The experiment also does not yet demonstrate:

- charge;
- mass-energy relation;
- spin 1/2;
- quantum behaviour;
- Lorentz invariance;
- electromagnetism;
- electron stability in physical units;
- electron/positron duality.

## Why this is still important

The direction is now correct. Instead of taking the known electron and decomposing its already-known relations, the experiment begins with a minimal medium and asks whether a stable entity can arise from relations.

The next experiment must therefore test the emergent structure itself:

1. introduce two emergent structures;
2. measure whether they interact;
3. classify attraction/repulsion/merger/scattering without naming the result;
4. search for conserved quantities generated by the dynamics;
5. test whether opposite configurations exist;
6. vary lattice scale and update rate;
7. test whether the structure survives perturbation;
8. repeat across independent generators to determine generator dependence.

Only after these properties emerge should we compare the resulting behavioural signature with known physical entities.

## Laboratory verdict

**EMERGENT ENTITY-LIKE STRUCTURE: FOUND.**

**ELECTRON: NOT FOUND.**

The experiment successfully demonstrates a minimal relation-generated localized propagating structure. It does not justify identifying that structure with an electron.

The next objective is not to force the structure toward electron properties. It is to determine which properties emerge naturally from the relation dynamics.
