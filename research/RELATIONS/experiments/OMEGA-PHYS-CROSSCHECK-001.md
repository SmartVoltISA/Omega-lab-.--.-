# Ω-PHYS-CROSSCHECK-001 — Relation classes vs first-generation fermion charge structure

Date: 2026-08-13
Status: CROSS-CHECK / HYPOTHESIS OPEN

## Question

Can the four minimal Ω relation classes be compared with the four first-generation matter fermions (u, d, e, ν_e) without identifying them by name or by numerical coincidence alone?

## Established physics input

First-generation matter fermions:

- up quark u: electric charge +2/3 e
- down quark d: electric charge -1/3 e
- electron e: electric charge -1 e
- electron neutrino ν_e: electric charge 0

Proton = uud; neutron = udd.

## Normalisation test

Choose the common charge unit q0 = e/3.

Then:

ν_e = 0 q0
d   = -1 q0
u   = +2 q0
e   = -3 q0

This permits an algebraic decomposition into integer units, e.g. +2 = +1 + +1 and -3 = -1 -1 -1. This is only an algebraic decomposition; it does NOT demonstrate physical constituents.

## Composite charge checks

proton = u + u + d = +2 + +2 - 1 = +3 q0 = +e

neutron = u + d + d = +2 - 1 - 1 = 0 q0

These reproduce the known electric charges of proton and neutron.

## Structural observation

The first generation is organised into two pairs:

(u, d): +2/3 and -1/3
(ν_e, e): 0 and -1

Within each pair, the electric-charge separation is one elementary charge in magnitude.

This is an observed structural fact of the Standard Model, not evidence by itself for Ω.

## Ω interpretation under test

Candidate interpretation:

- Ω has four minimal relation classes.
- A common unit q0 = e/3 may correspond to a more elementary relation quantity.
- Integer multiples of q0 may represent composite/derived relation states rather than distinct primitive entities.

This is a hypothesis only.

## Critical control

Do NOT conclude that u is physically composed of two +q0 constituents, or that e is physically composed of three -q0 constituents, merely because their charges have those integer representations.

Current particle physics treats quarks and electrons as elementary. Experiments continue to test possible substructure. No experimental evidence is currently established that the fractional-charge quarks are physically composed of smaller charged constituents.

## Required next test

Construct the Ω interaction/transition table independently, without importing particle names or known particle charges. Then compare that table with the experimentally established interaction properties of u, d, e and ν_e.

The comparison must record both matches and contradictions.

## Result

The common e/3 normalisation and the uud/udd charge sums are consistent with the proposed Ω bookkeeping, but they are NOT a derivation of particle physics and NOT confirmation of the Ω hypothesis.

Status remains OPEN.
