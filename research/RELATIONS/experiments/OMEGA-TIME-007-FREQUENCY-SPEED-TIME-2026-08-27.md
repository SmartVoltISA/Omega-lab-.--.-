## Research question

Test the proposed distinction:

> Time is fundamental; frequency and speed depend on the temporal rate of change.

Use the same sequence of distinguishable states and vary only the frequency at which transitions occur/present. Then perform the inverse construction: hold speed fixed and vary both transition size and frequency.

## Experimental construction

Let one transition correspond to a fixed spatial displacement:

`Δx = 0.25 m`

Test frequencies:

`f = 1, 2, 5, 10 Hz`

For periodic repetition:

`f = 1 / Δt`

and therefore:

`Δt = 1/f`.

For one transition, speed is:

`v = Δx/Δt = Δx·f`.

### Results — fixed transition size

| Frequency f (Hz) | Interval Δt (s) | Speed v (m/s) |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 1.0 | 0.25 |
| 2 | 0.5 | 0.50 |
| 5 | 0.2 | 1.25 |
| 10 | 0.1 | 2.50 |

For the same 10-transition sequence, total duration is respectively:

`10 s, 5 s, 2 s, 1 s`.

The sequence/order of states is unchanged; frequency changes the duration of traversal and therefore the measured speed.

## Inverse test

Fix target speed:

`v = 1 m/s`.

Choose frequencies `1, 2, 5, 10 Hz` and set:

`Δx = v/f`.

This gives transition sizes:

`1.0 m, 0.5 m, 0.2 m, 0.1 m`.

Thus the same speed can result from different combinations of spatial change and frequency/time interval.

## Interpretation

The experiment supports the following operational dependencies:

`frequency = cycles / time`

`speed = change / time`

and, for periodic transitions,

`v = Δx·f`.

Therefore frequency is not identical to time, and speed is not identical to frequency. Both are rates defined relative to a temporal measure.

The photograph analogy is reproduced structurally: the same two-frame state transition can be replayed at different frequencies. The state sequence remains the same while the apparent/assigned rate of motion changes.

## Critical control

The experiment does **not** establish that physical time is ontologically fundamental. It establishes only that once a temporal interval is admitted, frequency and speed are rates with respect to that interval.

The previous OMEGA-TIME-001–006 experiments showed that abstract ordered change can be represented without a physical-time variable. Therefore two interpretations remain logically open:

1. **Fundamental-time interpretation:** physical time is primitive, while frequency and speed are derived rates.
2. **Emergent/relational interpretation:** ordered change is more primitive, and physical time is a metric used to quantify it.

This experiment alone cannot distinguish these ontologies.

## Important result

The user's proposed separation is internally consistent:

`TIME → temporal interval → FREQUENCY`

and

`TIME → temporal interval + spatial change → SPEED`.

But the direction of ontological dependence is not established merely by the dimensional equations.

## Next decisive experiment

Construct two physically distinct autonomous processes with no shared external clock. Determine whether they can recover a common temporal scale from internal relational structure alone, or whether a common physical-time reference must be supplied.

This is the decisive test between a fundamental temporal background and a fully relational metric.
