# Ω-REL-036 — Four-State Dynamics

Date: 2026-08-13

## Question
Is the 3-cycle special to a three-state relation, or is it one member of a general N-state dynamical structure?

## Model
Let S contain N states and let f:S→S be a deterministic relation-evolution law.

For N=4 there are 4^4 = 256 possible laws.

## Full enumeration

All 256 functions were enumerated and their directed functional graphs classified by cycle lengths.

Cycle-structure counts:

- (1): 64
- (1,1): 48
- (1,1,1): 12
- (1,1,1,1): 1
- (1,1,2): 6
- (1,2): 36
- (2): 48
- (2,2): 3
- (1,3): 8
- (3): 24
- (4): 6

Total = 256.

## Key results

1. A 3-cycle is not unique to the three-state model.

For N=4:

- 24 laws contain a pure 3-cycle;
- 8 laws contain a 3-cycle plus a fixed point.

Thus 32 of 256 laws contain a 3-cycle.

2. A 4-cycle appears for the first time at N=4.

Exactly 6 laws produce a pure 4-cycle.

3. The maximum possible cycle length equals N.

Therefore the 3-cycle is not fundamentally special merely because N=3. It is the first nontrivial member of a general N-state cyclic possibility.

## Max-cycle distribution

- maximum cycle length 1: 125 laws
- maximum cycle length 2: 93 laws
- maximum cycle length 3: 32 laws
- maximum cycle length 4: 6 laws

Total = 256.

## Conclusion

The previous Ω-REL-035 result that three states permit exactly two oriented full 3-cycles remains correct, but it must not be interpreted as evidence that three is a unique universal number.

Ω-REL-036 shows that cyclic relational dynamics generalize: with four states, both 3-cycles and 4-cycles exist.

Therefore the current evidence supports:

- 3 is sufficient for a closed cyclic relation;
- 3 is not uniquely privileged by deterministic finite-state dynamics alone;
- the number of possible phases can grow with the state-space size.

This is a result about the tested finite-state model, not a claim about fundamental Ω ontology.
