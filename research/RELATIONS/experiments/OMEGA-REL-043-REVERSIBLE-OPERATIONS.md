# Ω-REL-043 — Reversible Operations and Minimal Generation

Date: 2026-08-13
Status: RESULT REGISTERED

## Question
Can the two generator roles previously called cycle and local permutation be reduced to a single more fundamental property, or are multiple reversible operation roles required?

## Model
For a finite state set S, consider only bijective transformations (reversible operations). These form the permutation group on S.

The test is separated from irreversible transformations. Irreversible state-merging is not used in this experiment.

## Exhaustive small-N check
For N=3 and N=4, all permutations were enumerated and generator subsets were tested by closure under composition.

Results:

- N=3: the full reversible space has 3! = 6 operations and requires at least 2 generators. A suitable pair generates all 6.
- N=4: the full reversible space has 4! = 24 operations and requires at least 2 generators. A suitable pair generates all 24.

Thus a single reversible operation is insufficient for N>=3, while two suitable reversible operations can generate the full permutation space for N=3 and N=4.

## Important correction
The two generators should NOT be treated as two fundamental properties of a relation. They are generators of a mathematical operation space. Different pairs can generate the same full reversible space.

Therefore the invariant result is not the identity of a particular generator, but the need for at least two suitable reversible operations to generate all permutations for N>=3.

## Structural distinction
The previous C/P pair can be interpreted as two complementary operational roles:

1. a nontrivial/global rearrangement capable of moving states through the space;
2. a local rearrangement that breaks the restricted cyclic symmetry and permits generation of the remaining permutations.

However, this is a characterization of one useful generator pair, not yet a fundamental ontology.

## Relation to irreversible operation
Reversible operations preserve the number of distinct states:

|f(S)| = |S|.

An irreversible merging operation satisfies:

|f(S)| < |S|.

Therefore reversibility alone cannot generate state-merging transformations.

This keeps two operational behaviours experimentally distinct:

- preservation of distinctions;
- destruction/merging of distinctions.

## Result
The reversible sector has at least two independent generator degrees of freedom for N>=3, but the specific generators are not unique.

The irreversible sector cannot be generated by reversible operations alone.

This does NOT establish a fundamental number of relation types. It establishes an operational separation between reversible rearrangement and irreversible loss of distinction, and a two-generator minimum within the reversible transformation space for the tested N.

## Next question
Test whether the operational distinction "preserve vs destroy distinction" survives changes of representation and whether reversible operations can be characterized by a smaller invariant property set without naming particular generators.
