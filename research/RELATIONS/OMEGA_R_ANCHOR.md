# Ω-R ANCHOR — RELATIONS FIRST

## Non-negotiable starting point

Ω-R does **not** start from nodes.

The research starts from **relations / connections** and asks what structures can emerge from them.

Canonical Ω-R direction:

`RELATIONS → STRUCTURES → EMERGENT NODES`

A node is not a primitive assumption. A node is a candidate higher-level structure produced by stable organization of relations.

## Use of conventional mathematics

Ordinary graph theory, mathematics and established physics are comparison and formalization tools. They are not the ontological foundation of Ω-R.

When conventional mathematics uses `G=(V,E)`, Ω-R must not silently inherit the assumption that `V` is primary. The notation may be used after the Ω relational structure has been defined, or as a control/comparison model.

## Ω comparison protocol

```text
Ω relation / observation / hypothesis
          ↓
compare with established mathematics or physics
          ↓
if already equivalent → retain the known formulation
if genuinely different → isolate exactly what is new
if unclear → preserve both descriptions
```

The purpose is not to reinvent arithmetic or established mathematics. The purpose is to investigate a domain where the **language of relations itself is the research object**.

## Emergence question

The central structural question is:

> Can stable configurations of relations produce the structures we later call nodes?

This remains an OPEN hypothesis and must be tested.

## Research sequence

1. Relations / connections.
2. Properties of relations.
3. Multiple relations and their organization.
4. Stable relational structures.
5. Edge as a formalized/structured relation where justified.
6. Emergent node-like structures.
7. Larger networks.
8. Propagation and dynamic restructuring.
9. Comparison with established mathematics and physics.

## Evidence rule

Do not import a familiar ontology merely because it is familiar. If the Ω result contains nothing new, retain the established description and move on. If something genuinely new appears, record **what is new, why it is new, and what evidence supports it**.

No claim becomes a fact because it fits an existing mathematical vocabulary.
