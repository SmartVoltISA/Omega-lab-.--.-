# Candidate Properties of an Ω Relation

## 1. Topological
- existence
- direction
- degree contribution
- connectivity role

## 2. Geometric
- length
- cross-sectional area
- thickness
- volume
- orientation

## 3. Mechanical / dynamical
- strength
- stiffness
- elasticity
- damping
- persistence
- breaking threshold

## 4. Informational
- capacity
- transmission rate
- delay
- mutual dependence
- memory / history dependence

## 5. Energetic
- creation cost
- modification cost
- destruction cost
- reversible vs irreversible cost
- energy stored in the relation

## 6. Density

"Density" remains deliberately undefined. Candidate operational definitions include:

- edge weight per unit length;
- relation capacity per unit volume;
- number of independent interaction channels per spatial volume;
- concentration of relational state.

These definitions must not be conflated.

## 7. Minimality question

For each property P, determine whether P can be represented as a function of other properties. If yes, it is potentially derived rather than fundamental.

## 8. Directional test

For a relation with an internal orientation, compare the minimum intervention cost for changes parallel and perpendicular to that orientation. The abstract result is a directional response function:

`C(theta)`

The special case `C(0) != C(pi/2)` is sufficient to establish anisotropy in the model.

## 9. Important distinction

Strength and stiffness are not synonyms.

- strength: threshold before failure / rupture;
- stiffness: response magnitude to deformation.

A relation can therefore be strong but compliant, or weak but stiff, depending on the operational definitions.
