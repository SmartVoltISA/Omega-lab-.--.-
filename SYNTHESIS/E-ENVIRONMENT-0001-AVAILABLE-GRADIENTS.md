# E-ENVIRONMENT-0001 — Environmental Gradient Inventory

## Purpose
Inventory real environmental differences that can act as a source for controlled relational-energy experiments.

## Candidate gradients
1. Atmospheric electric field: fair-weather near-surface field is about 100 V/m; fair-weather air-Earth current density is about 3 pA/m². This is a real potential gradient but a very weak power source. Source: Burns et al., JGR / DOI 10.1029/2004JD005469.
2. Temperature gradient: Seebeck effect converts a temperature difference into voltage. Bi2Te3-based materials show Seebeck coefficients on the order of ~100–200 μV/K depending on composition and sample. This makes ΔT a controllable test source.
3. Magnetic field: permanent NdFeB magnets provide strong static magnetic fields; common N35–N45 grades have remanence roughly 1.17–1.38 T and maximum energy products roughly 263–366 kJ/m³. A static field alone does not continuously supply power; changing magnetic flux or mechanical configuration is required for induction.
4. Triboelectric/contact electrification: contact-separation between dissimilar materials can produce opposite surface charges. Output depends strongly on material pair, surface, humidity, geometry and contact mode. This is experimentally useful as a mechanically driven charge-separation source.
5. Solar radiation: optical energy provides a large external gradient and can drive photovoltaic/electronic transitions.
6. Mechanical vibration/air motion: motion can repeatedly change geometry or magnetic flux and thereby drive electromechanical conversion.
7. Humidity/chemical gradients: material/environment interfaces can produce electrochemical or triboelectric differences; these need material-specific measurement.

## Practical ranking for first experiments
A. Triboelectric contact-separation — simplest bench test, cheap instrumentation.
B. Thermoelectric ΔT — clean quantitative relation V ≈ SΔT.
C. Magnet + coil — clean test of flux-change -> induced voltage, but requires controlled motion.
D. Atmospheric electric field — good environmental measurement, poor power source.
E. Solar — strongest practical environmental source, but less isolated as a fundamental gradient experiment.

## Important distinction
A potential/field existing in the environment is not equivalent to usable continuous power. A closed cycle can extract net energy only if an external gradient or reservoir replenishes the energy lost per cycle.

## Lightning analogue
Do not attempt to capture natural lightning in a jar/container. Use a controlled capacitor + high-voltage discharge experiment instead. The safe research analogue is: charge separation -> stored field energy -> threshold -> controlled discharge -> measured energy. Natural lightning is a high-energy atmospheric discharge and is not an appropriate improvised experiment.
