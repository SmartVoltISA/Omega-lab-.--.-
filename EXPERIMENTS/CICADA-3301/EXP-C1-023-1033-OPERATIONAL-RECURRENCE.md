## Question
Does `1033` recur in Cicada as an operational value across independent stages, rather than only as a repeated decorative number?

## Provenance-controlled observations

### 2013
The Cicada OS `prime_echo` script emits primes through 3301 and deliberately pauses longer at **1033** and **3301**. The later 2013 onion command set explicitly includes `get 3301` and `get 1033`. Separately, the physical-poster stage used phone numbers ending in **3301 or 1033**; callers then used the poster access code in gematrified form. These are operational uses, not merely printed numbers.

### 2014
A server-status/OOB artifact produced a **5×5 magic square with constant 1033**. The same matrix was later reproduced in Liber Primus-related material. The 2014 portrait extraction also produced two number columns whose sums were **1033** and **3301**; the documented OpenPuff extraction used the concatenated password `33011033` in one reconstruction. The historical record has a conflicting secondary transcription `33011103`, so the exact password provenance is retained as an evidence note rather than treated as a universal rule.

The 2014 LP delivery material also contains `CicaDOS 1.033` / `Cic/DOS/ 1.033` user-agent strings. This is an additional occurrence, but its semantic purpose is unresolved.

## Control: 3301
3301 is also operationally recurrent: it is the endpoint of the 2013 prime stream, a special pause point in `prime_echo`, appears in phone-number suffixes, is a magic-square constant, appears in PGP message sign-offs, and is embedded in the OpenPuff password.

Therefore the recurrence of 1033 alone cannot be treated as statistically exceptional without a larger corpus and matched controls.

`1033 → pause/state marker`  
`1033 → command/address selector`

`1033 → magic-square invariant`
