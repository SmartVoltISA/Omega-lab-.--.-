# E-LIGHT-0001 — Green / Light / Separation

## Question
Can the observed green state be treated as a neutral/balanced state in the Ω relational model, and can light be modeled as an operation that separates a relation before a later restructuring?

## Important correction
Green leaves are NOT evidence that nature defines green as a universal equilibrium state. Chlorophyll absorbs blue and red strongly and absorbs green less strongly; green light also penetrates deeper into leaves and is used in photosynthesis. The visible green is therefore a spectral consequence of selective absorption/transmission/scattering, not proof of balance.

## Working hypothesis
In the Ω model, green may be retained as the operational symbol for a low-conflict / near-balanced state. This is a model convention, not yet a physical law.

A separate hypothesis is:
LIGHT → separation / excitation → altered relation → possible charge separation → subsequent recombination or restructuring.

This has a real physical anchor: in photosynthetic reaction centers, photon absorption can drive photochemical charge separation. The experiment must not assume that all light universally "separates" a relation.

## Minimal toy test
Two coupled states x1,x2 with relation strength r. A light pulse temporarily weakens r and applies equal/opposite perturbation to x1,x2, preserving the mean state.

No-light control returns to x1=x2.
Light condition creates an antisymmetric split during the pulse and leaves a nonzero separated state immediately after the pulse in the toy model.

Example run: after the light interval, |x1-x2| ≈ 1.54 while control remains 0.

## Status
PRELIMINARY / TOY MODEL ONLY.
The numerical result demonstrates only that a defined separation operator can produce separation. It does NOT demonstrate a physical law of light.

## Next test
Replace the imposed light operator with a spectral/energy-dependent interaction and test whether separation emerges from the relation dynamics rather than being manually injected.
