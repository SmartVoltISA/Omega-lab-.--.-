# Ω-RH-32 — COMPACT-RESOLVENT REPLACEMENT FOR SICHE
Date: 2026-08-26
Status: PROMISING OPERATOR ROUTE / KEY LEMMA TO PROVE / NO RH CLAIM

## Objective
Replace the failed claim that the prime shift operator P_λ itself is compact with the stronger and more appropriate question: does the full Weil operator QW_λ have compact resolvent because its archimedean part is confining and the prime/polar terms are bounded perturbations?

## Source structure
The Siche repository explicitly decomposes

    QW_λ = A + Π − P,

and its construction represents A as a diagonal Fourier multiplier with symbol proportional to the derivative of the Riemann–Siegel theta function. The same repository separately treats Π as the polar term and P as the finite prime-sum operator.

The audited script therefore gives the right decomposition for a replacement argument, even though its Krein–Rutman compactness claim for P is rejected in RH-31.

## Candidate operator theorem
Let H be the stated even-sector Hilbert space and let A be the self-adjoint realization of the archimedean Fourier multiplier

    (Af)^hat(t) = a(t) f^hat(t),

with

    a(t) = theta'(t)/pi

up to the fixed normalization used by QW_λ.

The asymptotic of the Riemann–Siegel theta derivative is logarithmic:

    a(t) = (1/(2*pi)) log(|t|/(2*pi)) + O(t^(-2))

as |t| -> infinity, up to the normalization convention.

Hence a(t) -> +infinity. If the exact domain is chosen so that A is self-adjoint and its multiplier tends to +infinity, then

    (A + cI)^(-1)

is compact for every c above the lower bound. This follows directly from the Fourier multiplier: the resolvent multiplier 1/(a(t)+c) tends to zero at infinity, and its level sets are finite-measure / compactly embedded in the corresponding form domain.

## Bounded perturbation test
For fixed λ, the prime sector contains only finitely many prime powers q <= λ². Each truncated translation is bounded on L², with operator norm at most 1 under the natural partial-isometry normalization. Therefore a crude bound is

    ||P_λ|| <= 2 * sum_{q=p^m <= λ²} log(p) q^(-1/2).

The polar term Π_λ is finite rank (in the displayed Fourier construction it is generated by the two evaluation functionals at ±i/2), hence bounded whenever those functionals are continuous on the declared form/domain space.

Therefore, if these domain statements are established exactly,

    B_λ := Π_λ − P_λ

is a bounded self-adjoint perturbation of A.

Standard bounded-perturbation/resolvent identities then give compact resolvent for

    QW_λ = A + B_λ,

provided A has compact resolvent and QW_λ is self-adjoint on D(A).

This is the key replacement:

    P_λ noncompact        -> accepted obstruction (RH-31)
    A compact resolvent   -> candidate confining mechanism
    Π_λ − P_λ bounded     -> candidate perturbation
    QW_λ compact resolvent -> discrete spectrum

## What this WOULD solve
A valid compact-resolvent theorem would give:

1. discrete spectrum of QW_λ;
2. finite multiplicity of every eigenvalue;
3. existence of a lowest eigenvalue when QW_λ is bounded below;
4. a legitimate spectral framework for attacking S1.

It would NOT by itself give simplicity. Simplicity still needs an independent argument (positivity/irreducibility, variational strict convexity, a nondegenerate ground-state theorem, or another mechanism).

It would also NOT establish S2 (convergence to Xi) or RH.

## Critical checks
The following must be proved before promoting this from candidate to theorem:

### C1 — exact Hilbert space
Identify whether the operator acts on L²([-log λ, log λ]), a form completion, or the full logarithmic line. The compact-resolvent statement depends on the exact space and domain.

### C2 — exact archimedean symbol
Derive the precise multiplier a(t) from the Weil form, including constants and behavior near t=0. The only required asymptotic property is a(t)->+infinity, but the lower bound must be controlled.

### C3 — self-adjoint domain
Show that A is self-adjoint on its natural multiplier domain and that Π_λ−P_λ is A-bounded with relative bound 0 (boundedness is sufficient for fixed λ).

### C4 — polar evaluation functionals
The quantities f_hat(±i/2) must be continuous in the actual Hilbert/form topology. If they are not continuous on H itself, move to the closed form domain and prove the corresponding form-bounded estimate.

### C5 — boundary/cutoff consistency
The finite-cutoff translation convention must agree with the domain used for A. No hidden boundary term may be lost when passing between the Fourier and logarithmic representations.

### C6 — uniformity in λ
Compact resolvent for each fixed λ is not enough for S2. A separate λ->infinity convergence theorem is still required.

## Why this branch is worth pursuing
The numerical source itself already separates A, Π and P, and its A matrix is diagonal in the Fourier basis. The failed compactness of P therefore does not destroy the entire spectral construction. It points to a more standard operator-theoretic route: let the unbounded archimedean part provide compactness of the resolvent, while the arithmetic terms act as bounded perturbations.

This is materially different from the rejected Krein–Rutman premise and can be tested analytically without relying on finite-grid regression.
