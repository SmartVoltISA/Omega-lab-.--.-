# Ω-RH-30 — SICHE / KREIN–RUTMAN ROUTE AUDIT
Date: 2026-08-26
Status: CRITICAL GAP IDENTIFIED / NO RH CLAIM

## Target
Audit the new Siche route claiming that the prime-sum operator P_λ is positive, compact and irreducible, so Krein–Rutman gives simplicity of the dominant eigenvalue and hence simplicity of the smallest eigenvalue of QW_λ.

## External source
Public repository: Architect-Resonance/Riemann-Hypothesis.
The README describes S1 = spectral simplicity and S2 = convergence as the two missing CCM conditions, and states that P_λ is positive, compact and irreducible. The repository provides reproducible numerical scripts.

## Finding 1 — compactness is not established by the supplied script
The script `krein_rutman_test.py` explicitly says the continuum prime operator has a delta-shift kernel and then asserts that it is compact / finite-rank because it is a finite sum of shifts on a compact interval. This is not a valid functional-analytic argument.

A translation/shift operator on L² of an interval is generally bounded but not compact. A finite sum of such composition/shift operators does not become compact merely because the interval is compact. The delta-supported kernel is also not Hilbert–Schmidt. Therefore the script's compactness conclusion cannot be promoted to a theorem without a separate operator-theoretic proof.

## Finding 2 — irreducibility is only conjectured in the script
The same script explicitly observes that T(delta_j) has many zero entries and therefore the discretized operator is not strongly positive. It then suggests that iterates might become strongly positive and invokes density of prime logarithms as the essential mechanism. No proof is supplied that the actual positive semigroup generated by the truncated prime shifts is irreducible on the continuum Banach lattice in the required sense.

The statement that rational independence / density of logarithms of primes is enough is not, by itself, a proof of irreducibility of this particular positive operator with boundary truncation and one-sided positive weights.

## Finding 3 — finite numerical tests do not establish Krein–Rutman hypotheses
The test uses a finite grid and finitely many nonnegative test functions. That verifies a discretization, not the continuum operator. The script itself labels the strict-positivity condition PARTIAL and concludes that simplicity is 'effectively guaranteed' from a numerical near-rank-1 gap. 'Effectively guaranteed' is not a mathematical substitute for irreducibility or a direct simplicity theorem.

## Finding 4 — asymptotic exponent fits are not asymptotic proofs
`even_sector_scaling.py` fits power laws numerically and then concludes that if the fitted exponent of gap(P_even) exceeds that of ||Pi_even||, the gap is positive for sufficiently large λ. A finite log-log regression does not establish an asymptotic exponent bound. To close the route one needs explicit inequalities with constants valid for all sufficiently large λ.

The script then combines finite numerical checks with the fitted asymptotic behavior and labels the resulting gap 'UNCONDITIONAL'. That implication is not justified by the supplied computation alone.

## Finding 5 — fixed Fourier dimension does not establish the continuum limit
Several scripts keep N fixed (for example N=15 in `polar_norm_scaling.py` and `commutator_test.py`). Increasing λ while holding Fourier dimension fixed can test a finite matrix family, but it does not prove convergence of the underlying operators on the infinite-dimensional Hilbert space. A separate uniform-in-N argument is required.

## Finding 6 — commutator test is numerical, not a proof of [P,Pi] -> 0
`commutator_test.py` evaluates ||[P,Pi]|| for seven finite λ² values at fixed N=15. A decreasing sequence over seven values cannot establish a limit theorem. The normalized commutator is useful evidence but still needs an analytic bound tending to zero with λ and control uniform in the Fourier truncation.

## Consequence
The Siche route has produced a useful structural decomposition and several testable numerical observations, but the supplied verification layer does not establish the two gates it is intended to close:

S1: simplicity of the continuum QW_λ ground state.
S2: convergence of the ground-state eigenfunction to Xi.

In particular, the Krein–Rutman application cannot yet be accepted because compactness and irreducibility of the actual continuum prime operator remain unproved in the supplied material.
