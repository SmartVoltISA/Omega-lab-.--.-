# Ω-B0 — Initial hypothesis: “self-organizing will”

## Status

**Superseded / partially falsified.**

This record preserves the original experimental motivation rather than rewriting history after the controls.

## Question

Can a dynamical field generate persistent domains and transitions from an internal mechanism that was initially interpreted as “spontaneous will”?

## Initial observations

The first visualization showed:

- a bimodal distribution of `u`;
- apparent domain formation;
- high `|∇u|` near domain boundaries;
- apparent domain births near `u = 0`;
- different boundary statistics between several mechanisms.

## Initial interpretation

The mechanism was tentatively described as “spontaneous will”. This was a hypothesis label, not a demonstrated physical or philosophical property.

## What the controls changed

Ω-B2 showed that the persistence of many domains depended strongly on a non-standard diffusion rule. Therefore, the number of persistent domains cannot by itself be interpreted as evidence for an emergent agency or will.

Ω-B1 nevertheless showed a potentially useful distinction between structured internal dynamics and matched white noise.

## Current conclusion

The original claim “self-organizing will” is **not supported** by B0 alone.

The surviving research question is narrower:

> Can structured internal dynamics produce reproducible statistics that differ from equivalent random noise, after architectural and null-model controls?

That question is now studied as Ω-B1 and later controls.
