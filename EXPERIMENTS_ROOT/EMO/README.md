# Ω-EMO — Exploratory Emotion / Information-State Models

## Status

**HYPOTHESIS / NOT ESTABLISHED**

This branch records a research idea: whether human emotional states can be modeled as information-bearing state transitions with characteristic directions of action.

## Initial candidate mappings

These are hypotheses, not definitions or scientific facts:

- fear → withdrawal / avoidance / preservation of an existing model;
- aggression → directed action / overcoming resistance;
- interest → movement toward the unknown / information acquisition;
- curiosity → reduction of uncertainty;
- humility → acceptance of model error / reduction of unjustified certainty;
- anger → detection of mismatch between expected and observed state;
- joy → signal of favorable state correspondence or goal attainment;
- grief → registration of loss of an established state or relation.

## Minimal proposed cycle

`state → difference → evaluation → internal state → action tendency → state change`

The goal is to determine whether apparently different emotions can be represented by a smaller common set of variables, rather than assigning one independent variable to every emotion.

## Spectrum / basis hypothesis

A second, separate hypothesis asks whether information-bearing states may have a measurable multidimensional "spectrum" or decomposition.

The starting intuition is that a small number of independent basis components might generate a larger family of observable states through combination and relative strength. This is a research hypothesis, not an established fact about information.

### Physical color control

Human color vision provides a useful empirical analogy, but not a proof of the information-spectrum hypothesis. Typical human color vision begins with three cone classes (L, M, S) with overlapping spectral sensitivities; later neural processing compares cone signals through opponent mechanisms. Human perception also has four commonly described unique hues: red, green, blue and yellow. citeturn0search0turn0search3

Therefore the first Ω question should not be "how many colors exist?" but:

> **What is the smallest experimentally supported basis of independent channels, and what larger set of states can be generated by their combination?**

For physical light/color, candidate basis descriptions must be kept separate:

- 3 receptor channels: L/M/S;
- opponent axes: red–green, blue–yellow, plus luminance/black–white in common opponent descriptions;
- perceptual unique hues: red, green, blue, yellow.

These are different layers of description and must not be collapsed into "three fundamental colors" or "four fundamental colors." citeturn0search1turn0search7

## Candidate Ω research variables

For the abstract information-state model, begin with the smallest candidate basis rather than assigning one dimension to every emotion:

1. difference / distinction;
2. uncertainty;
3. direction of state change (approach ↔ withdrawal);
4. action intensity / magnitude;
5. model confidence / stability.

These variables are provisional and may be reduced or rejected.

## First experiments to design

1. Define a minimal state space without assuming semantic labels are fundamental.
2. Determine whether 2, 3, 4, or more independent variables are actually required to reproduce selected state transitions.
3. Use null models where labels or directions are randomized.
4. Measure whether combinations of a small basis reproduce a larger set of observed states.
5. Test whether a "spectral" representation adds predictive power over a simpler representation.
6. Attempt to break the mapping before expanding it.

## Rules

- No psychological diagnosis from this model.
- No claim that emotions reduce to energy, information, or color.
- No claim that "information spectrum" is physically equivalent to optical spectrum.
- Separate FACT, OBSERVATION, INTERPRETATION, HYPOTHESIS and UNKNOWN.
- All empirical claims require execution evidence under `METHOD.md`.
