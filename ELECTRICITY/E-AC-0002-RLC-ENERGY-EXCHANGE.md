# E-AC-0002 — RLC Energy Exchange

## Question
Does zero instantaneous/average voltage at some moments imply zero stored potential? Can energy move between different forms of relation without disappearing?

## Minimal model
Series RLC with sinusoidal excitation. State variables: capacitor charge q, inductor current i. Stored energies:

- capacitor: U_C = q²/(2C)
- inductor: U_L = Li²/2
- resistor dissipation: P_R = Ri²

For R=0, total stored energy U_C + U_L is conserved and oscillates between electric-field and magnetic-field forms. For R>0, stored energy decays only through the resistive channel.

## Control expectation
At instants where capacitor voltage crosses zero, capacitor energy is zero but inductor energy can be maximal. At instants where current crosses zero, inductor energy is zero but capacitor energy can be maximal. Therefore a zero in one observable does not mean zero system potential.

## Ω interpretation (hypothesis, not established physics)
A system can pass through a neutral observable state while retaining potential in another relational degree of freedom. A transition may be interpreted as redistribution/reconfiguration rather than disappearance.

## Result
The ideal RLC equations confirm the energy exchange exactly: energy oscillates between U_C and U_L when R=0; with R>0, the decrease of stored energy equals resistive dissipation. The neutral crossings are not stop states.

## Important correction
This does NOT establish that energy is merely a consequence of link rearrangement. It establishes only that stored energy can migrate between coupled degrees of freedom and that zero of one state variable is not zero of the whole system.
